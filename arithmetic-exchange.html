A logical-induction market in facts about numbers

The Arithmetic Exchange

Each day the exchange lists new facts about numbers: is this one prime, is that digit of π a 3, does this number take long to fall under the Collatz map. Traders bet on them using simple heuristics, and a slow prover settles each bet a few days later. The prices are the market's probabilities, set by LIA-fast so that no trader here can exploit them.

    Prices are probabilities

    Every day the exchange finds prices at which the combined, budget-limited bets of all traders make almost no money in any outcome. That is the fixed point at the heart of logical induction, and it makes each price an honest probability with respect to the traders on the floor.

    Heuristics compete

    Traders that keep losing burn through their budgets and lose their say. The Optimist is gone within weeks. Lucky sevens fades more slowly, because each of its mistakes is small. The sieves overtake the prime number theorem, because they know more about each number.

    Two priors

    The exponential prior is the original paper's: trader k starts with weight 2−k and each budget b with 2−b, so losers are written off fast. The heavy-tailed prior is LIA-fast's: 1/(k(k+1)) and slowly decaying budget weights. It guarantees that a good trader far down a long list is heard within polynomially many days, but on a small floor like this one it is slower to give up on a loser.

    Computable facts snap to 0 or 1

    Collatz facts are cheap to compute, so the trader that simply runs the sequence takes over and those prices go to 0¢ or 100¢. Switch on Miller–Rabin or Computes π in the roster to see the same happen for primes and digits. With them off, π's digits stay near 10¢: the right answer for a reasoner who cannot compute them.

    Prices come from LIA-fast, the faster logical induction algorithm (Garrabrant et al., arXiv:1609.03543, §5, with the speedups described in the companion write-up). The prover is simulated: it checks each fact with an exact computation and announces the answer a few days after listing. Larger numbers take longer.
